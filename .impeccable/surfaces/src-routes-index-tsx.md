Scope: the public landing page `/`. Visitor mode: Persuade.

Audience: someone already tracking money badly in a spreadsheet, often holding two currencies,
sceptical because every other product in the category wants a bank login. Action: create a free
workspace. Proof available: the product's own primitives rendered with static props. No social
proof exists and none may be invented (PRODUCT.md, Evidence on Hand).

## Direction contract

THESIS: The page opens on the refusal no competitor can make and spends the rest proving what
arrives instead. It refuses the category default: a centred hero over a browser-chromed dashboard
shot, then a grid of equal icon cards, then logos and pricing.

OWN-WORLD: DESIGN.md unchanged. Warm paper, two voices, hairlines, flat at rest. Composition is
what changes: bands that swap ground (canvas / sunken / panel) instead of repeating one well of
white cards, and real product primitives instead of illustrations of them.

STORY: This is a real ledger, manual by choice; rows arrive by fast entry, import, series and
rules; the arithmetic is right and shown; nobody ever asks for a bank password. So: start free.

FIRST VIEWPORT: No eyebrow. The refusal as the h1 at hero scale, split across two tones - the
claim in ink, the promise that it will not be walked back in the quieter one - on a sunken ground
ruled like an account book; the sentence naming what the product is sits beside it; one brand
action paired with the anchor that answers the refusal; and directly under them the ledger itself,
crossing the band boundary into the section that explains it. The promise and the thing the
promise is about are both above the fold.

FORM: "The Refusal", index 3 of seven ordered structures, dealt as the lead. Seed key efcbd655.
Bands alternate ground rather than repeat one well; rows alternate the side the product sits on;
the page closes on an inverted inset panel rather than on another band of paper.

FINISH: unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the
verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance.
